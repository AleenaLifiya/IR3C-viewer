Real or reconstructed?

Some of these MRI volumes were fully acquired. In the others, only one slice in every 2, 4 or 8 was kept, and the rest were reconstructed by IR3C. Scroll through each volume and decide. Your answers stay in your browser: nothing is sent or recorded.

Slice mask: uniform sampling. With a factor of R, every R-th slice is kept (the first and last slices always), the kept slices are shown exactly as acquired, and only the slices between them are reconstructed.

Reconstructions are produced by IR3C, described in “Anchor, then correct: what makes missing-slice MRI reconstruction robust to the slice pattern” (under review). Slices were removed synthetically from complete research scans; acquired slices are kept unchanged. All subjects are from the held-out test split.

These are research images, not for clinical use. Intensities are shown as 8-bit images, windowed at each volume's 99.5th percentile.

Data: BraTS 2023 adult glioma challenge (validation release); OAI-ZIB knee MRI, derived from the Osteoarthritis Initiative; IXI-tiny, a downsampled version of the IXI dataset (brain-development.org/ixi-dataset, CC BY-SA 3.0). IXI-tiny is downsampled to 83×44×55 voxels (about 2×4×4 mm), so its two examples look coarse whether real or reconstructed.
